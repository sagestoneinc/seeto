How we evaluate a deal

Rent comparables first

Projected rent is the input every other number depends on. We use what comparable units are actually leasing for, not optimistic pro formas.

Real carrying costs

Texas property tax rates are high relative to much of the country, and insurance has moved sharply. Both belong in the model before you make an offer.

Capital expenditure reserve

Roof, HVAC, and water heater have known lifespans. A return that ignores replacement reserves is not a return.

Vacancy and turnover

Turnover costs money in lost rent, make-ready work, and leasing fees. We model a realistic vacancy rate for the specific submarket.

Exit assumptions

How the property is likely to resell matters as much as monthly cash flow, particularly for shorter holding periods.

Financing structure

Investment loans price differently than owner-occupied. The financing you can actually obtain shapes which deals make sense.

Investment questions

What returns should I expect on a Texas rental?

It varies widely by submarket, property age, and financing. Any specific figure quoted before analysing an actual property is guesswork. We build the numbers for the property in front of you and show you the assumptions.

How do Texas property taxes affect returns?

Texas has no state income tax but comparatively high property tax rates, and rates vary meaningfully between taxing jurisdictions. This is one of the largest line items in a Texas rental model and a common reason out-of-state investors overestimate returns.

Do you work with out-of-state investors?

Yes. Seeto Realty works with investors buying into the Dallas-Fort Worth and Houston markets from elsewhere, including coordinating inspections and walkthroughs remotely.
